Facebook and Instagram for churches: a pastor's guide.

Seven in ten American adults use Facebook, and about half use Instagram. The families who live near your church are in that crowd. An invitation from your pastor can reach them on the phone they already hold.

Talk to the family who has never visited

Most church pages speak to members: the potluck sign-up, the youth trip. A neighbor who taps your page for the first time came with other questions:

  • The service times
  • The plan for their kids
  • The dress code, in one line
  • The length of a service

Plenty of neighbors are asking. In a Pew Research Center report from 2016, about half of American adults said they had looked for a new congregation at some point, most often after a move. Among adults under 30 who had searched, 59% looked for information online.

Set up your Page and profile for guests

  • Name. Match the sign out front, so a search for your church finds the Page.
  • Profile photo. Use a logo people can read at thumbnail size.
  • Cover photo. Show real people at your church, in a photo from this year.
  • Service times and address. Put them in your Page's intro and the first line of your Instagram bio.
  • A plan-your-visit link. Add it to your Page's details and your Instagram bio. The bio holds up to five links, so put this one first.
  • A pinned welcome. Pin your pastor's 30-second invitation to the top of both.
  • Messages. Answer within a day. A question about service times comes from a family deciding where to go on Sunday.
  • A professional account. Switch your Instagram to one. It's free, and it shows how many people each post reached.

A posting rhythm a small staff can keep

Start with two posts a week: a look inside on Wednesday and an invitation on Friday. Add the others once that rhythm holds.

Four habits help every post:

  • Film upright. A vertical video fills the phone screen, and both apps show Reels to people who don't follow you yet.
  • Put the words on screen. Plenty of people scroll with the sound off. Instagram's free Edits app adds captions for you.
  • Keep words out of the top fifth and the bottom third. The apps' buttons sit there. Our free Reel safe zones file has the measurements.
  • Show your own people. A shaky clip of your greeters says more than stock footage of a church that isn't yours.

The invitation: 30 seconds from your pastor

One video carries most of the load: your pastor, looking at the camera, inviting families to one service at one time. It needs four lines.

  1. A name and a church. "Hi, I'm Pastor Daniel at Cedar Bend Church."
  2. An invitation. "This Sunday, we're saving a seat for your family."
  3. A time and a plan for kids. "Join us at 9:00 or 10:45. Kids have their own classes."
  4. One ask. "Tap the button and tell us you're coming."

Our guide to filming a church invitation video on a phone covers the setup and a script you can copy.

A personal invitation still does the most. Lifeway Research asked 2,000 unchurched Americans in 2016, and 51% said a personal invitation from a friend or family member would be effective in getting them to visit a church. A Facebook ad drew 18%. So make your ad feel personal. Put your pastor's face on it, and ask every member to share it with one friend.

Show it to families who live close

A post on your Page reaches a slice of the people who already follow you. Paying Meta, the company behind both apps, puts your invitation in front of neighbors who have never heard of your church.

Keep the circle tight. A family 10 minutes away can come back next Sunday. In Ads Manager, drop a pin on your building and set a radius around it. A 10-minute drive covers about 3 to 6 miles in town and more on country roads.

Meta treats that circle as a firm limit. It shows your invitation to people who live inside it or were there recently.

Leave the rest of the audience open. Meta removed ad targeting tied to religion in January 2022, so church ads reach people by location and age.

One rule to know before you write the ad

Meta bars ads that say or imply something about a viewer's religion. Its own example of a line that breaks the rule is "Are you Christian?" Write about your church and its Sunday, and leave the viewer's beliefs out of it.

Boost a post, or use Ads Manager?

The Boost button under a post is the fastest start, and it suits a small push before a big Sunday.

Ads Manager takes longer to learn and gives you more control. You can set firm start and end dates and run several videos side by side. After a week, pause the videos families scroll past and keep the one they tap.

New to Ads Manager? Pick the Traffic goal first. It sends people to your plan-your-visit page and needs no extra setup. Once your website carries Meta's tracking pixel, switch to the Leads goal, and Meta will look for people who finish the form.

Budget: small and steady

Spend a little every day for a month. $10 to $20 a day keeps your invitation in front of the same neighbors more than once, and it gives Meta time to learn who responds. Our own plans include $500 a month, about $16 a day.

A burst of $300 over one weekend buys a spike, then silence. Check on a steady campaign twice a week and leave it running between checks.

Send every tap to a plan-your-visit page

A home page makes a guest hunt. A plan-your-visit page answers the first questions on one screen: service times, your address, the plan for kids and a button to save a seat.

Keep the form short. Ask for a name, a cell number, the service they picked and their kids' names.

Then follow up like a host. Our guest follow-up guide covers the week after a first visit, with texts you can copy.

Count families

Likes and views feel good. You still won't know who walked in. Each week, write down four numbers:

  1. Dollars spent
  2. Families who filled out your plan-your-visit form
  3. Families your greeters met on Sunday
  4. Families who came back

At the end of the month, divide the dollars by the families you welcomed. Your elders can weigh that figure against any other outreach the church funds.

Other apps can wait

In Pew Research Center's 2026 survey, 70% of American adults said they use Facebook and 51% said Instagram. TikTok came in at 37% and Snapchat at 26%. Among adults 30 to 49, the ages of many parents with kids at home, 78% use Facebook and 64% use Instagram.

Start with Facebook and Instagram. One ad account covers both, and one upright video fits both. Once your weekly rhythm holds, post the same video to YouTube Shorts and TikTok. Snapchat can wait.

Questions pastors ask

Can churches run Facebook and Instagram ads?

Yes. A church runs ads from its Facebook Page the same way a business or a nonprofit does. Meta removed targeting tied to religion in January 2022, so churches reach people by location and age.

Is boosting a post enough?

For a small push, yes. Move to Ads Manager once you want firm dates or more than one video running.

Do we need professional video?

No. A phone by a window and 30 seconds from your pastor make a good invitation. Our filming guide shows the setup.

Should a small church be on both Facebook and Instagram?

Yes, and it takes little extra work. One upright video fits both, and one ad account runs on both.

Does our pastor have to be on camera?

Your pastor makes the strongest invitation, since a family meets that same face on Sunday. A warm staff member or a longtime member works too.

Is it right for a church to advertise?

We see an ad as an invitation with a longer reach. Your members' invitations still matter most, and they can share the same video with a friend.

Church guest follow-up: the first seven days.

A new family walked through your doors on Sunday. They found a parking spot and the kids' rooms, then sat through a whole service among strangers. The week ahead is your chance to thank them and ask them back.

Follow-up starts with a name

You can't thank a family you can't name. Some churches call them visitors. We say guests, since a guest is someone you were expecting. Most churches already ask for a name. In a 2024 Lifeway Research survey of 1,003 Protestant pastors, 80% said their church has a printed card for guests to fill out, and 38% have an online form.

Ask for four things:

  • First and last name
  • Cell number
  • Kids' names and ages
  • A box to tick: "Yes, text me about my visit."

Put the same four on a card at every seat and on a page families can open from a QR code. Guests fill out a short card. They leave a long one on the seat, so save the mailing address and the prayer requests for later.

Families who planned their visit online have already answered. Skip the card for them and greet them by name. Our free plan-your-visit page checklist covers that online form.

Sunday morning: five moments a guest remembers

Follow-up works best after a good Sunday. Pew Research Center surveyed Americans who had looked for a new congregation, and 79% said feeling welcomed by leaders played an important role in their choice. Only the quality of the sermons ranked higher, at 83%.

Walk through your own Sunday the way a stranger would:

  • The parking lot. Mark a few guest spots near the main door.
  • The front door. One greeter says hello and learns the family's name.
  • Kids check-in. A parent's whole morning rides on this handoff, so walk the family to the room.
  • The seat. A greeter walks the family in and introduces one person nearby.
  • The walk out. Someone who met them says goodbye by name, and your pastor stands by the doors.

Lifeway's survey found 16% of churches ask guests to stand during the service. We'd skip that. A guest came to see your church, and standing puts the whole room's eyes on them.

Before the family leaves, your greeter writes down two details: the kids' names and one thing the family mentioned. Monday's note needs both.

The seven days, one step at a time

  1. Sunday afternoonText a thank-you, with a link to four short questions about their morning.
  2. MondayYour pastor mails a handwritten note. Add the family to your list.
  3. TuesdayRead their survey answers, and reply to anything personal the same day.
  4. ThursdayInvite them back. Name the service time and offer to save seats.
  5. SaturdayText the families who said yes, with parking and kids check-in details.
  6. SundayGreet them by name, and introduce them to one more person.

That adds up to two texts and one note, plus a reminder for families who said yes. None of them asks for money or a commitment.

Don't wait for the staff meeting. Thom Rainer of Church Answers puts "The time is short" first on his list of six keys for guest follow-up. A text on Sunday afternoon arrives while the kids are still talking about their class.

Texts you can copy

Send these from a number in your church's name. Swap in your details, then add one line only your team would know, like the kids' names.

Sunday afternoonTo every first-time family
Hi [First name]! It's [Church name]. Thanks for coming this morning. We loved meeting your family. Got a minute? Four quick questions about your first Sunday: [link]
ThursdayTo every first-time family
Hi [First name]! It's [Church name]. We'd love to see you again this Sunday at [time]. Want us to save seats for your family? Reply YES and we'll have them ready.
SaturdayTo families who replied YES
Hi [First name]! It's [Church name]. We saved your seats for [time] tomorrow. Park in the guest spots by the front doors. Kids check-in is inside on your right.
A month laterTo families who haven't come back
Hi [First name]! It's [Church name]. We start a new series this Sunday at [time], and we thought of your family. We'd love to see you. Reply YES and we'll save seats.

Texting rules in the United States

  • Text only the people who gave you their number and said yes to texts.
  • Stop the day anyone replies STOP. The wireless industry's own guidelines call for both.
  • Sending from texting software? Carriers make your church register first. Look for A2P 10DLC in your provider's settings, and file early. Approval takes anywhere from a few days to three weeks.
  • Answer every reply the same day.

Your texting provider can confirm the rules for your number. Our free first-visit follow-up texts file drafts these messages in your church's voice.

A four-question survey for first-time guests

A short survey gets you two answers a handshake won't: your Sunday through a newcomer's eyes, and the next step a family wants.

First Sunday surveyLink it in Sunday afternoon's text
1. Rate your first Sunday with us, from 1 to 5. 2. Tell us one thing that made you feel welcome. 3. Tell us one thing we could do better. 4. Pick a next step, if you'd like one: meet the pastor, hear about kids ministry, try a small group, or nothing yet.

Build it in any free form tool. Four questions take about a minute to answer.

Read every answer on Monday morning. A complaint about parking or kids pickup is a gift, since members stopped seeing those problems years ago. Once a month, share the answers to question 3 with your team and leave the names off.

Want more replies? Offer a small thank-you, like a coffee card, to families who finish it.

The note from your pastor

Rainer's list also includes "The power of the handwritten note." Mail yours on Monday so it lands midweek. Keep it short. Thank the family, and mention one detail from Sunday that proves a person wrote it.

A sample noteSwap in the details your greeter wrote down
Dear Sarah, Thank you for bringing your family on Sunday. Ava and Leo were a joy in our kids' class, and I hear Leo built the tallest tower in the room. I'd love to say hello next time. Look for me by the front doors. Pastor Daniel

No mailing address yet? Send the same words as an email from the pastor. A digital card works too, as long as a person writes the message.

A small gift, if you give one

Lifeway's survey found 41% of churches have a gift for guests. Keep yours small and useful, like a coffee card or a book for the kids. Hand it over in person at your welcome spot, so a volunteer learns the family's name.

Leave the stack of brochures out of the bag. Your follow-up text gives the family one clear next step, and one is enough.

Put one person in charge

Follow-up breaks at the handoffs. A card rides in an usher's pocket until Wednesday, and nobody knows whose job the text was.

Name one owner. By 2:00 on Sunday afternoon, that person has every card and every form in one list. A shared spreadsheet does the job for most churches, with these columns:

  • Family name
  • First visit
  • Kids' names and ages
  • Greeted by
  • Sunday text
  • Note mailed
  • Invited back
  • Came back

Pay for texting software once your list outgrows what one person can text by hand.

After the first week

  • They came back. Greet them by name. After a second visit, offer one next step, like a small group or coffee with the pastor.
  • They didn't come back. Wait a month, then send one more invitation with no guilt. Tie it to something new, like a sermon series.
  • Christmas and Easter. Send a holiday invitation to each family who agreed to texts. Our Christmas Eve ad guide covers that season.
  • They replied STOP. Take them off the list that day.

Count the families who come back

Write down four numbers each month:

  1. First-time families
  2. Families who gave you a name and a cell number
  3. Families who came back within a month
  4. Families who took a next step

Use the second number to judge your card, and the third to judge your follow-up. Bring all four to your elders, with the names behind them.

Questions pastors ask

Is Sunday afternoon too soon to follow up with a guest?

No. Their morning is still fresh, and a thank-you asks nothing of them. Send the text between 1:00 and 3:00, after lunch.

Is a text too forward for a first-time guest?

No, as long as the family gave you their number and said yes to texts. Keep it short and warm, and ask for one small thing at a time.

Should the pastor call every guest?

A call from the pastor means a lot, and many people don't pick up for a number they don't know. Send the text and the note first. Then offer a call and let the family choose.

Can we text guests from a staff member's cell phone?

Use a number in your church's name. The thread stays with the church if that staff member leaves, and your whole team can see who replied.

Do we need software for guest follow-up?

No. A spreadsheet and one church phone number cover a small church. Texting software helps once the list outgrows one person, and in the United States it needs carrier registration first.

Should we keep texting a family who didn't come back?

Send one more invitation a month later. After that, invite them at Christmas and Easter if they agreed to texts. Stop the day anyone replies STOP.
